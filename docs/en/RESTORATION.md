## Why I rebuilt the Mach series

There was someone named [mesabloo](https://github.com/Mesabloo/hm-defense) who worked on a similar project. He obtained the assets for HeavyMach Defense and wrote the source code from scratch. He appears to have stopped while implementing combat between units. I had a better starting point than he did, since he had to write the source code from the ground up.

One weekend, I had a dream. I was watching myself in the third person as I encountered the iPod touch and Cydia for the first time. After waking up, I searched Google for heavy Mach. Gemini in Google Search pointed me to the [Internet Archive](https://archive.org), where I could find the original HeavyMach. I began the restoration with HeavyMach 1.13 from its [HeavyMach series collection](https://archive.org/details/heavy-mach-archive_202406).

That is why I think I did almost nothing. It is also why I think I have even less reason to consider making money from this. What I did, in my own words, was grab Codex by the collar and slap it when it strayed from the original source code and the creators' aesthetic achievements and went off doing things wrong on its own.

Development was my third career. In 2015 I worked as a magazine journalist, and in 2016 I worked in the Philippines. In July 2017, I became an iOS developer. The world I encountered in work far removed from software was nondeterministic and irrational to me. There were several reasons I left that life and chose another occupation. Over glasses of soju, friends sometimes tell me that if I had continued the work I did in 2015 and 2016, I would have ended my life by jumping to my death. I still do not know whether they are right.

I think HeavyMach accounted for about 35% of my decision to become a developer. That experience also influenced my choice to make a Unity game based on HeavyMach's core concept instead of copying Lineage, Counter-Strike, or the 3D games that followed. (The Unity game is a separate project from restoring the original HeavyMach. This repository concerns the restoration of the original HeavyMach.)

Events at the magazine had an even greater influence. Editorial designers with skills that were difficult to replace collectively defied management, and their working conditions improved as a result. They secured the right to leave on time except for the two days after the monthly deadline. I wrote about this in [“The Price of Defiance”](https://sass.studio/magazine/cerulean). Interviewing executives at factory-automation companies and hearing about the benefits engineering brings also influenced my decision to become a developer.

My original idea was to restore every game in the Mach series by developer Hae-jun Byun and illustrator Jae-cheol Park. The titles I knew were HeavyMach1, HeavyMach2, and MachDefense. I began with HeavyMach1, the game that had influenced my career.

The response was unexpectedly good. I received my first-ever PR on a public repository. Someone else spent their own time adding code to a game I had restored. I wanted to restore the next title too.

If I eventually create an App Store account for sass.studio and the original creators and stakeholders permit distribution, it will still be free.

In series order, HeavyMach2 should have come first. But in my memory, HeavyMach2 did not have many chapters. I remember the first chapter, Skyway, Sea Side, and canyons and mountains. I do not remember the exact names. I wondered whether more chapters had been planned as updates. At the time, it felt like a demo game.

Its systems were complex. It had top-down combat, lock-on, jump evasion, and demanding leveling. It attempted many things within the technology of the time and a small mobile game. I wondered whether I could finish restoring it. So I chose MachDefense first.

To me, MachDefense belongs to the twilight of the Mach series. For [mesabloo](https://github.com/Mesabloo/hm-defense), who attempted its restoration before I did, I understand it was a game that colored his childhood. His attempt had a major influence on my decision to restore HeavyMach1 and MachDefense. I do not know whether he knew HeavyMach1 and 2 as well.

## I have no intention of making money from this restoration

When writing my developer's statement, I write out my thoughts, but I also sometimes ask Codex to interview me. Codex asked which scene in the restored game brought back old memories. My answer is “Everything.”

I do not have much time left. I have no intention of making money from this. If a stakeholder contacts me, I will take this repository down without regret.

Trying to make money from this project would dishonor the software that showed me a new path as a developer, and the work of the developer and illustrator who made it. That gives me even more reason not to think of profiting from it.

If I eventually create an App Store account for sass.studio and the original creators and stakeholders permit distribution, it will still be free.

I have no intention of making money from this restoration. I want to publish the code and assets together so that others can build and play it themselves. I also want to leave a record of how I restored the game and what I fixed.
If a stakeholder contacts me, I will take this project down. The 9 years and 3 months that HeavyMach's creators gave me exceed the lifespan my Creator originally allotted to me.

Money is only needed in this life.

## Developer's statement — closing my final defense

I close my final defense as a developer with a passage from Mikhail Bulgakov's The Master and Margarita and words of my own.

Platforms perish and the body returns to earth, but a will that has been forged does not weather away.
By making the heart of the software that kept me alive beat again, deep beneath ruins the world has forgotten,
I leave my time here.

Manuscripts don't burn.
